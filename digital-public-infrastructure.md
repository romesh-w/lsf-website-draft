Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) is the set of shared digital systems that a country builds once so that everyone can use them: things like digital identity, payments, secure messaging and data exchange between government agencies.

Think of it like roads or the electricity grid. The government builds the foundation, and public services, businesses and citizens build on top of it. Done well, DPI makes services faster, cheaper and fairer for everyone.

## Why it matters

DPI decides how a country's digital life works: who controls the data, who can build on it, and whether it can be trusted. Countries that build strong, open foundations can innovate on their own terms, instead of depending on systems controlled by a few large companies or foreign powers.

## How LSF helps

LSF builds DPI as free and open source software, so that Sri Lanka and any other country can use it, inspect it, adapt it and run it themselves, without licence fees or lock-in.
